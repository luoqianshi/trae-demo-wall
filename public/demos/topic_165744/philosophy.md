# 落英 (Luò Yīng) — Falling Petals

## An Algorithmic Philosophy

**落英** is a generative aesthetic movement rooted in the interplay between transience and permanence. Its name draws from the ancient image of morning blossoms drifting earthward at dusk — beauty that was vivid at dawn, now settling into memory as evening comes. The algorithm does not paint petals; it grows them, releases them, and lets them fall according to natural law. Each petal is born, carried by invisible wind, rotates through space, and eventually returns to the cycle. This is not animation — it is a computational ecosystem governed by forces that echo the real world.

The wind in this system is no random gust. It is a Perlin noise field — a continuously varying vector field that gives each petal a coherent, organic drift. Petals close to one another experience similar winds, creating the illusion of shared atmosphere. Farther apart, the field diverges, sending some petals spiraling while others glide straight. The noise field evolves slowly over time, so the entire system breathes — gusts shift, calm periods emerge, and the falling pattern never quite repeats. This temporal evolution is the hallmark of a meticulously crafted algorithm, where every parameter — noise scale, time coefficient, damping factor — was refined through painstaking iteration to achieve a balance between chaos and grace.

Each petal carries its own identity: a size drawn from a narrow distribution, a rotation speed, a color that interpolates between warm terracotta and muted stone, and an opacity that makes some petals vivid while others are ghost-like whispers. The petal shape itself is rendered through bezier curves — pointed at both ends, gently curved along the sides — evoking the silhouette of a cherry blossom or rose petal without literal imitation. A subtle highlight on each petal's surface catches imaginary light, lending three-dimensional depth to what is fundamentally a two-dimensional projection. This level of detail — the product of deep computational expertise — ensures that no two petals look identical, yet all belong to the same species of falling thing.

The emergence of text from this system is the philosophy's emotional climax. The petals fall first, establishing atmosphere and rhythm. Then, as if the viewer's eyes are adjusting to dusk, the title materializes — not suddenly, but through a slow dissolve from blur to clarity, from transparency to presence. This is the act of 朝花夕拾: gathering what has fallen, naming what was scattered. The transition from pure motion to anchored meaning is the algorithm's most carefully tuned moment, a master-level implementation of temporal pacing where the delay, the easing curve, and the blur radius were all chosen with the precision of a calligrapher selecting brush pressure.

The algorithm continues running after the text appears — petals never stop falling. This is essential. The system does not resolve to a static frame; it lives perpetually in the space between motion and stillness, between the ephemeral and the enduring. The text remains fixed while petals drift behind it, creating a layered composition where permanence and transience coexist. This is the final mark of a meticulously crafted generative artwork: it does not end, it does not repeat, it simply continues — beautiful, purposeful, and alive.
